Audit of book-engine at commit `28ea1af`, 2026-09-20, run the day the first register closed at 81 of 81. Rust backend, React frontend, Python import, the seam between Rust and TypeScript, the repository's own tests, your real vault, and your real cache database.

**26 findings:** Critical 2, High 6, Medium 10, Low 8.

Two more were raised and **disproved**; they are at the end, under [What was looked at and was not a fault](#not-a-fault), because knowing a thing was checked is worth as much as knowing it was wrong.

### <a id="cq-08"></a>CQ-08: The import puts 52 words in your book that the book does not contain

- [x] Fixed in `55c1d9d`, `4baa778` and a re-import of the book on 2026-09-20  |  Severity: Critical  |  Proof: Counted in your real vault  |  Effort: Medium  |  Fix order: step 1
- **Half done in `55c1d9d`.** The rule stopped guessing, so no import can do this again, and the function has its first 12 tests. Measured on the real book: of 56 damaged paragraph starts, the old rule glues back all 56 and the new rule glues back none. **The words already in the book are untouched.** That is the re-import, and it is a change to real vault data.
- **The import check sees this now, in `4baa778`**, which closes the evidence line above saying nothing downstream repairs it. `book_problems` refused only missing anchors, dead footnote links and broken characters; it now also refuses a book that starts a paragraph with a word the book writes nowhere else. Measured: 0 flagged across 2,144 paragraph starts of two books that came in from EPUB, 56 in this book and 6 in the other PDF one. `audit-anchors.py` on the real vault names all 56, chapter by chapter, so the re-import can prove itself instead of being taken on trust.
- **The book itself is repaired, on 2026-09-20.** The words were taken out the only way they can be: the PDF was imported again, with `--force`, after the vault was copied to two zips on two disks and both were read back. **56 invented words to 0, `book_problems` 7 to 0, glued-letter damage 58 to 0.** The book grows from 7 parts to 16 and from 74,114 words to 80,602, because IN-01's front and back matter come back with it. **Nothing was lost**, measured three ways, because one probe per paragraph had already reported a loss that was not there: the word bag is 110 words short and every one of them is a scar leaving; every 60-letter run of the old book was checked against the new one and all 103 breaks hold a scar and no ordinary word; all 133 pictures are present by sha256, next to 7 new ones. Your saved work moved with its chapters (IN-04) and was checked file by file: the bookmark keeps `^p-003` in its new `ch-05.md`, the 17 reading rows keep 992 seconds, the note keeps its 196 bytes and its hash. **The cost is 3 rows of `reviews.jsonl`**, which survive as rows but no longer name a card that exists, because card ids are keyed to the text and the text is what changed. A rehearsal on a copy of the real vault ran first, and the book written into the vault is byte for byte the book the rehearsal made.

**What happens.** Paragraphs in the Dalton book begin with words that are not words. The reader sees them on screen, search indexes them, and any quote taken from such a paragraph carries them.

**Why.** `heal_drop_caps` repairs the large decorative capital that opens a chapter in a printed book. It does it two ways, and both guess.

- **Rule 2** joins any single capital at a paragraph start to the lowercase word after it. That is right for a drop cap. It is wrong for the two one-letter words English paragraphs most often begin with: **A** and **I**.
- **Rule 3** finds a capitalised word further into the paragraph, **moves that capital to the front**, and leaves the word behind without it. One firing, two scars.

**Evidence.**

- **52 damaged paragraph starts**, counted by taking each paragraph's first token, splitting off its first letter, and keeping it only when the book uses the rest as a word and never uses the whole token. Seventeen different capitals have been glued on: A 19, P 7, F 6, N 3, D/C/O/J 2 each, and M, I, S, Y, E, T, H, L, V once each.
- The unmistakable ones are the short words: the book writes `the` 5,682 times and has never written `Cthe`, `Fthe`, `Nthe` or `Pthe`; it writes `to` 2,099 times and has never written `Yto`, `Eto` or `Ato`.
- **Rule 3 is proved by correlation, not inference**: in twelve places a glued capital at the line start pairs with a beheaded word on the same line. A text extractor cannot produce that pattern.
- Rule 3 carries a hard-coded list of words to spare — Texas, London, York, America, England, France, California — which is the code admitting it eats proper nouns.
- Nothing downstream repairs it. `book_problems` checks anchors, footnote links and replacement characters only, so a chapter full of these passes the import check and reaches the vault.

**Where.**

- `packages/ingestion/ingest/pdf_sanitizer.py:54` (rule 2) and `:57-79` (rule 3), reached from `sanitize_pdf_markdown` at `:155`.

**How to fix.** The repair this rule needs already exists one module away: `ingest/text_repair.py:76` `letter_that_fits` restores a letter only when exactly one capital turns the stem into a word the chapter itself uses. Rule 3 should ask it instead of guessing, and rule 2 should refuse to glue `A` or `I`. A test for `heal_drop_caps` is the first thing to write; there is none today.

**What the attack found.** The attacker tried to pin the damage on four other modules — the PDF library, `glyph_repair.py`, `text_repair.py`, `layout_stitcher.py` — and failed on all four, then supplied the twelve-pair correlation above. **It also found the count was too low**: the first count was 25, the attacker's was 39, and the measured answer is 52.

**Fixing the rule does not fix the book.** The 52 words are in `vault/books/` now. Repairing them means re-importing that book, which moves the reader's notes with their chapters and is a change to real vault data.

**Related:** [CQ-07](2026-09-14-findings.md#cq-07), which fixed one page of this by supplying the right letter and left the guessing rule running.

### <a id="cq-09"></a>CQ-09: A short or picture-only page vanishes from the book without a word

- [x] Fixed in `ecc8b0a`  |  Severity: High  |  Proof: Reproduced by reading the code  |  Effort: Small  |  Fix order: step 1
- **The length is gone, not reported.** Reporting a dropped page still leaves it out of the book, and the machinery that decides what a page is worth was already there and already right: `holds_no_text` holds a title page over for the chapter it introduces, and a page of a picture or a table is a chapter and stays one. The twenty letters stood in front of all of it and answered first. A page that makes no block at all is now named on the way past, in the shape the notes branch uses, and **every document of the spine is counted against what became of it**: a name that leaves the loop with no reason stops the import rather than writing a book quietly shorter than its source. The orphan picture goes with the page it belongs to. 11 new tests, **6 of them red against the old code**, and the comment in the five test files that worked around the gate is replaced.

**What happens.** An EPUB document whose text is under twenty characters, or which yields no blocks, is skipped. Its chapter file is never written and its contents entry is dropped, so a frontispiece, a dedication, an epigraph page or a one-line closing chapter is simply not in the book.

**Why.** Two bare `continue` statements with no message.

**Evidence.**

- Ten lines above, the same loop **prints** why a notes-only document made no chapter, which is what IN-07 asked for. Two lines later it says nothing.
- Nothing compares the source spine against the chapters written. `audit-system.py` never opens the source file, and its `spine` is the import's own output, so the comparison is circular. `audit-anchors.py` delegates to `book_problems`, which iterates the chapter files that exist.
- The image is extracted into `assets/` before the document dies, and the EPUB path has no orphan cleanup, so the file sits there referenced by nothing.
- **The test that sounds like the defence is not one**: `test_a_page_of_a_picture_stays_a_chapter_of_its_own` builds a plate page with a caption far over the twenty-character gate. Five test files carry the comment "a page of under 20 letters is dropped as blank" — the suite works around the behaviour instead of pinning it.

**Where.**

- `packages/ingestion/ingest/pipeline.py:143-146` and `:154-155`
- `packages/ingestion/ingest/book_check.py:54-84`

**How to fix.** Say which document made no chapter and why, in the shape the `only_notes` branch already uses. Then make `book_problems` compare the number of spine documents against the chapters written, so a silent drop fails the import instead of shortening the book.

**What the attack found.** Five attacks, all failed. The attacker confirmed the asset orphan and that IN-07's reporting stops one branch short.

**Related:** [IN-07](2026-09-14-findings.md#in-07).

### <a id="cq-10"></a>CQ-10: A book that ends on a title page loses that page, and a test hides it

- [x] Fixed in `908b28f`  |  Severity: Medium  |  Proof: Reproduced by reading the code  |  Effort: Small  |  Fix order: step 1
- **They had nowhere else to go, so they are a part of the book.** The holder is drained after the spine loop into a chapter of its own, named after its own title, with no practice card, because a title holds no sentence and that is the same reason such a page is no chapter while a chapter still follows it. It also corrects a reason that had stopped being true: a closing title page told the CQ-09 count it "waits for the chapter it introduces" when no chapter was coming, so the count named a document that made no chapter and gave a false reason for it. **The test was worse than the fault and is rewritten**, and 11 new tests sit in a file of their own, **6 of them red against the old code**, with a sight check on every one that searches, so none can pass on an empty read.

**What happens.** Headings from a document that holds only a title — `BOOK V`, an afterword title, a colophon — are held back to be attached to the chapter that follows. When no chapter follows, nothing drains them, and those words are in no file.

**Why.** `HeldOverBlocks` is emptied only by `take_names()`, which is called only inside the per-chapter branch. The spine loop ends and the held blocks are never consulted again.

**Evidence.**

- `docs/rules/ingestion.md` promises the opposite: "its headings go to the top of the chapter they introduce, so no word is lost."
- The contents entry goes too: such an entry gets an empty href, and the link cleaner drops an entry with neither href nor subitems.
- **The test is worse than the fault.** `test_a_title_page_with_no_chapter_after_it_loses_no_word` asserts that the words are **absent** from the chapter file. Its second assertion compares `total_words` against the sum of the per-chapter counts it was built from — true for any input whatsoever. The test asserts the loss and its name hides it.

**Where.**

- `packages/ingestion/ingest/pipeline.py:164-166` and `:241`
- `packages/ingestion/ingest/chapter_shape.py:76-109`
- `packages/ingestion/tests/test_chapter_shape.py:302-315`

**How to fix.** Drain `held_over` after the spine loop into a final chapter part, the way a trailing notes document is handled. Then rewrite the test to look for those words somewhere in the vault, and give it a sight check so it cannot pass on an empty search.

### <a id="cq-11"></a>CQ-11: A word split across a line keeps its hyphen

- [x] Fixed in `eebe32c`  |  Severity: Low  |  Proof: Confirmed in your real data  |  Effort: Small  |  Fix order: step 1
- **The list was one module away and unused.** `close_cut_words` asks `layout_stitcher.WORD_ENDINGS` now, the same list `the_words_prove_the_join` already asks about the halves of a sentence a page break cut. It is asked, not copied, so the two cannot drift apart. `JOINING_WORDS` is still asked first, and a test checks that no joining word is a word ending. The import log counts a hyphen kept and a hyphen taken off apart, because a person reads it (CQ-09). **776 hyphenated words of the real book were turned back into the cut shape and put through the new rule: 0 lose their hyphen**, and the control carries a sight check so that 0 cannot mean the rule fires on nothing. 11 new tests, 9 red against the old code.
- **What it does not cover, measured after the fix.** The evidence below was read from the older build of the book. Since the 2026-09-20 re-import the shape this rule catches occurs **0 times**. **2 words** of the current book are still hyphenated where the book writes them whole, and neither tail is a word ending, so this fix leaves both. Catching those needs the whole book's word counts, and `sanitize_pdf_markdown` only ever holds one chapter. Raised as its own task, with the larger fault found beside it, and not in this register.

**What happens.** A word broken by a hyphen at the end of a printed line is rejoined with the hyphen kept, so a word the book writes whole arrives hyphenated.

**Why.** `close_cut_words` cannot tell a real compound (`consumer-generated`) from a soft-hyphenated word, so it keeps the hyphen for both.

**Evidence.** Exactly **one** occurrence in the whole Dalton book, against 650 legitimate lowercase hyphenated compounds.

**Where.**

- `packages/ingestion/ingest/text_repair.py:29` and `:64-73`

**How to fix.** `layout_stitcher.WORD_ENDINGS` already lists `ness`, `tion`, `ment`, `ing`, `ity`, `ance` under the comment "None of these is an English word". Consult it the way line 69 already consults `JOINING_WORDS`. All four compounds in the existing test keep their hyphens, because none of their tails is in that set.

**What the attack found.** The attacker **weakened this hard**. The stated reason — that two modules disagree — is wrong: they take different inputs and both behaviours are deliberately tested. But it then broke the defence too: the discriminator exists one module away and is unused, so the trade-off never had to be made.

### <a id="ds-15"></a>DS-15: Lose the cache and your card schedules do not come back

- [ ] Fixed  |  Severity: Critical  |  Proof: Reproduced by reading the code  |  Effort: Medium  |  Fix order: step 2

**What happens.** After `index.db` is deleted, damaged, or carried from another machine, the first start puts back the review history, the heatmap and the reading time — and **not the card schedules**. Every mastered card is offered as new and due.

**Why.** `restore_progress_blocking` only ever runs `UPDATE fsrs_cards ... WHERE card_id = ?`. It never inserts a row. It runs inside the setup closure, before the app has ever built the deck, so the table is empty and the update matches nothing.

**Evidence.**

- The only two inserts into `fsrs_cards` are `upsert_card` and `restore_archived_card`, both reachable only through `sync_practice_deck_blocking`, whose single production caller is an IPC command the frontend makes after mount.
- `upsert_card` writes the literal `0, 0.0, 0.0, now, 0, 0` for state, stability, difficulty, due, last review and reps. `due_cards.rs:65` puts every `reps = 0` card in the **New** queue, and `submit_card_review_blocking` schedules purely from the `fsrs_cards` row — it never reads `review_logs`.
- The archive is no help: `fsrs_cards_archive` is created empty in a new database.
- `README.md:18` states the opposite outright: "puts your card schedules, review history and reading time back from those log files at the next start. Nothing is lost."
- The start-up message will print "0 cards rescheduled" beside a non-zero review count.

**Where.**

- `apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/db/restore.rs:127-152`
- `apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/lib.rs:74`
- `apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/db/deck_sync.rs:85`, `apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/db/due_cards.rs:62,65`

**How to fix.** Make the restore insert a card row when none exists, or run it after the first deck sync rather than before. The first is safer: the vault log holds everything the row needs.

**What the attack found.** Six attacks, all failed. **One real narrowing**: a second start does repair every card the reader did *not* practise in the first session, because `last_review` defaults to 0. A card they do practise appends a newer standing that supersedes the saved one, and that loss is permanent.

**Guard.** `db/restore_tests.rs:378` looks like it covers this and does not: it calls the deck sync **before** the restore, the opposite order to the app. Every other restore test pre-seeds the table with a helper. No test runs the restore into an empty `fsrs_cards`.

**Related:** [DS-01](2026-09-14-findings.md#ds-01), whose guarantee this is.

### <a id="ds-17"></a>DS-17: Closing the window throws away what the reader just typed

- [ ] Fixed  |  Severity: High  |  Proof: Reproduced by reading the code  |  Effort: Small  |  Fix order: step 2

**What happens.** The reader finishes a sentence in Chapter Reflections, the status button says "Saving...", and they close the window. The words are gone.

**Why.** Every flush in the app hangs off a React unmount cleanup. Closing the window destroys the webview, and those cleanups never run.

**Evidence.**

- There is no `beforeunload`, `pagehide`, `visibilitychange` or `unload` anywhere in `apps/desktop/src`, and no `on_window_event`, `CloseRequested`, `WindowEvent` or `Destroyed` anywhere in `apps/desktop/src-tauri/src`. Both searches return nothing.
- Only two Tauri plugins are declared, and neither does this.
- `lib/notesAutosave.ts:22` says flush exists because waiting for the timer "lost the last words **when the app closed**". The mechanism was built. Nothing calls it at that moment.

**Where.**

- `apps/desktop/src/components/NotesPane.tsx:102-107`, `apps/desktop/src/App.tsx:47`
- `apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/lib.rs`

**How to fix.** One `on_window_event` handler for `CloseRequested` that tells the page to flush, or a `pagehide` listener in the page that flushes every saver. The savers already exist.

**What the attack found.** The attacker confirmed all of the above and **made the loss bigger**: the autosave timer is cleared and restarted on every keystroke, so a reader typing faster than one key every 800 ms never triggers a save at all. The loss is not 800 ms — it is **everything since their last pause**. It also named three more: the settings saver at 400 ms, the synthesis notes at 800 ms, and the in-progress reading session, which is recorded only in an unmount cleanup.

### <a id="ds-16"></a>DS-16: A file that cannot be read looks like a file with nothing in it

- [ ] Fixed  |  Severity: High  |  Proof: Reproduced by reading the code  |  Effort: Small  |  Fix order: step 2

**What happens.** Five places ask `Path::exists()` whether anything is saved. That returns false for **any** metadata error, not only a missing file, so an unreadable file reads as "nothing saved yet". The syntopicon then hands back an empty topic, and its save has no read-back guard, so one save could replace a whole topic with an empty one.

**Why.** `Path::exists` is `fs::metadata(..).is_ok()`. The standard library's own documentation says a permission error returns `false`.

**Evidence.**

- `vault/json_store.rs:23` is the module whose stated purpose is that a damaged file must never read as empty. Its parse path is guarded — that was the DS-04 fix. Its stat path is not.
- The blast radius is wider than the syntopicon: preferences, the bookmark, the inspectional blueprint, the analytical notes and the highlights all read "nothing saved yet" from that one probe, and the read-back guards in three of those call the same function, so they are no-ops in exactly this case.
- **The codebase already guards this hazard twice, deliberately.** `book_is_in_vault` counts anything but `NotFound` as present, for LC-02. `is_taken` uses `try_exists().unwrap_or(true)`, with the comment "or when that cannot be told", for DS-12. Five other sites answer the same question the unsafe way.

**Where.**

- `apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/vault/json_store.rs:23`
- `apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/vault/syntopicon.rs:104` and `:204-211`
- `apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/vault/reader.rs:76`, `study_log.rs:134`, `highlights.rs:87`

**How to fix.** Use `try_exists()` and treat an error as "present", exactly as `is_taken` already does. Give `save_syntopic_topic` the read-back guard every other vault writer has.

**What the attack found.** The attacker **broke the mechanism first named**. A Windows sharing violation cannot do this: Rust's `fs::metadata` opens with zero desired access, which is exempt from share-mode checks. What survives is access-denied and cloud-placeholder errors, which the repository itself anticipates: `reader.rs:104` says a file "can be locked or offline for a moment". It also narrowed the syntopicon wipe: a persistently unreadable file is never offered by the list, so the wipe needs a *transient* failure that clears before the save. And it found one site much milder than claimed: a falsely empty reading log cannot delete cached time, because the backfill requires the log to be at least as large as the cache.

**Related:** [DS-04](2026-09-14-findings.md#ds-04), [DS-12](2026-09-14-findings.md#ds-12), [LC-02](2026-09-14-findings.md#lc-02).

### <a id="ds-18"></a>DS-18: A damaged ledger is replaced by a one-line ledger

- [ ] Fixed  |  Severity: Medium  |  Proof: Reproduced by reading the code  |  Effort: Small  |  Fix order: step 2

**What happens.** An unreadable `vault/_ledger.json` reads as an empty list, and the next import writes that back plus its one new record. The record of every book ever taken in — id, hash, original filename, intake date, chapter and word counts — becomes a single line.

**Why.** `read_ledger` returns `[]` for any `OSError` or `ValueError`, and the inbox takes that as the ledger.

**Evidence.**

- The Rust side of this same project states the rule verbatim and obeys it: a damaged file must never read as empty, "because the next save would write the empty data back and every saved item would be gone (DS-04)". It keeps a `.corrupt-<time>` copy. The Python ledger has no equivalent.
- The unsafe behaviour is **asserted as correct** by `tests/test_ledger.py:108-111`.

**Where.**

- `packages/ingestion/ingest/ledger.py:36-39`
- `.agent/skills/process-inbox.py:139` and `:198-201`

**How to fix.** Give the Python ledger the same treatment as the Rust vault files: keep a dated copy of the damaged bytes, and refuse to write a shorter ledger over a longer one without saying so.

**What the attack found.** **Four of five sub-claims fell.** The write is atomic (`_ledger.tmp` then replace); the file is tracked in git, so `git restore` recovers it to the last commit; only the inbox path can do this, not the two pipeline entry points; and it needs a genuinely new book importing in the same run. `audit-system.py` also detects the aftermath. What survives is the missing `.corrupt` copy, the missing re-read, and the test that blesses it.

**Related:** [DS-04](2026-09-14-findings.md#ds-04), [CQ-05](2026-09-14-findings.md#cq-05).

### <a id="ds-19"></a>DS-19: An incomplete book folder loses that book's history from the cache

- [ ] Fixed  |  Severity: Low  |  Proof: Reproduced by reading the code  |  Effort: Small  |  Fix order: step 2

**What happens.** A book folder whose `_meta.json` is missing is skipped in a way that marks the book as gone, and the indexer then deletes that book's review log rows and reading sessions from the cache.

**Why.** The `continue` at `indexer.rs:94` leaves `saw_every_book` true, so the book never enters the map handed to `set_aside_removed_books`.

**Where.**

- `apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/db/indexer.rs:94` and `:108-111`
- `apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/db/removed_books.rs:46-48`

**How to fix.** Treat a folder with no readable `_meta.json` as present-but-unknown, the way an unreadable one is already treated at `indexer.rs:95-100`.

**What the attack found.** **The trigger first named is dead.** The Python import builds in `vault/.import/` and renames the old folder *aside* before renaming the new one in, so during that window the folder is **absent**, not incomplete. A locked `_meta.json` does not do it either: any error but `NotFound` keeps the book. What remains are the slow triggers — a half-synced folder from a cloud client, a hand-made folder, or one left by a pre-IN-05 failed import, which `ingest/reimport.py:91` says outright can exist. **The consequence is recoverable**: the vault log is untouched, cards are archived rather than deleted, and an existing round-trip test proves everything returns at the next sync and restart.

**Related:** [LC-02](2026-09-14-findings.md#lc-02), [IN-05](2026-09-14-findings.md#in-05).

## <a id="not-a-fault"></a>What was looked at and was not a fault

Both of these were raised, written up, and then **disproved by the second pass**. They are recorded so nobody spends the time again.

### The search-hit markers are guarded

Two private-use characters must match between `db/search_text.rs:16` and `lib/searchSnippet.ts:4`, or search highlighting breaks and the raw characters reach the reader. It was reported that no cross-language test held them together.

**There is one, and it is in the file that was cited.** `db/search_text.rs:252-262` reads the TypeScript file with `include_str!`, renders the exact expected line for both constants, and asserts the file contains it. It runs in `cargo test`, which runs in CI.

**Why the mistake happened, because it matters more than the finding:** only `packages/ingestion/tests/` was searched for a cross-language guard, nothing was found there, and an absence was reported. The line was visible in an earlier search of the Rust file. **A search of the wrong place is not evidence of absence** — the same shape as several real findings above.

Residue, and it is minor: the pin is a literal source-text match, so reformatting `searchSnippet.ts` fails the Rust test with a message about formatting rather than about drift.

### The three unlisted backend calls are covered

`lib/apiFailures.test.ts:53` names 37 of the 40 functions that call the backend. `chooseVaultFolder`, `getBookVocabulary` and `getVaultStatus` are absent, and it was reported that their failure paths were untested.

**All three are tested elsewhere.** `lib/vaultGate.test.ts:89-108` injects a backend error, asserts `chooseVaultFolder` rejects, and asserts the reason reaches the reader — stricter than the loop it was compared against. `NotesDrawer.test.tsx:164` covers `getBookVocabulary`, which is the only one of the three with code after the call. And there is exactly one wrapper, so there is no per-function swallow path to test.

Residue: nothing enforces that the hand-written list stays complete, so a 41st call would not be added to it. That is list rot, not a coverage hole.
